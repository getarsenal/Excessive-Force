Kind of problem: a cantilever standing on a shell. The twist: the minaret's
lowest storey is four corner piers with an arch through each face, so two
hundred metres of tower stands on four points inside the hall's corner; the
hall's roof lies on beams on seventy-eight columns and comes down a bay at a
time, but the fight is the piers.

Measured: 11,096 stones at low, 24,244 at high, 0 loose at both. The worst
stone is a pier stone under a lintel end at 0.79 of capacity. The arches were
eleven metres wide first and their lintels handed a quarter of the minaret to
one pier stone each; at six metres, with the pier stones laid at the wall's
coarser grain, it holds — and one round into a pier is the level.
