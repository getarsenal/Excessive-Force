# Edinburgh Castle, Edinburgh — the proportion table

Filled in from three reference photographs and `python3 tools/survey.py edinburgh`
before the builder is written; checked against `node tools/blocks.mjs edinburgh` and
`node tools/postcard.mjs edinburgh` after.

The bake cuts Castle Rock as a crag and tail: the summit at 130 m ASL, level
(±1.5 m) over about 220 × 115 m, the first shelf 49 m down at a radius of
115–160 m, the second 85 m down beyond 175 m, Princes Street Gardens 85 m
below the north rim. The survey names thirteen of the castle's buildings on
the summit with their footprints and heights — the Royal Palace 23 × 41 at
15.3 m, the Great Hall 27 × 14 at 13, the Queen Anne Building 21 × 39, the
War Memorial chapel 38 × 26 at 14.5, St Margaret's 10 × 6 at 8, the Gatehouse
14 × 35 at 12.7, the New Barracks 56 × 63, the Governor's House 23 × 36 — and
the builder lays every one of them where the survey puts it. The Half Moon
Battery is not in the survey (it is a wall, not a building) and is drawn from
the photographs round the Palace's east end.

Stones: 11,328 at low (3,269 of them under the summit), 19,461 at high; 0
loose at both. Sections: halfmoon 1,746, greathall 966, barracks 828, palace
476, batteries 431, gatehouse 273, chapel 78.

The One O'Clock Gun is the level's `turret`, on Mills Mount behind the
western battery wall at (−99, −40) in world metres, on the summit's own level
ground, firing north.

Kind of problem: fortress on a hill, the Potala's. The twist: the Half Moon
is a retaining wall — a battered D of stone thirty metres from the Esplanade
to its parapet — with the battery's fill and the Palace's ground behind it,
and nothing on the rock leans or topples; it is a siege, course by course.
