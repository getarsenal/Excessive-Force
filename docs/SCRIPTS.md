# Stand-off scripts, by country

Three beats before every level: the General's ultimatum, the defender's
refusal, the General's last word. The defender speaks his own language where
the writer put it there; the General never understands a word of it.

Lines live in `src/game/cast.js` under `STANDOFF` (the catalogue's, levels 42
to 100, in `WORDS` in `src/game/atlas.js`); the cast (ranks, names, nations,
portraits) is `CAST` in the same file. The first nine are the writer's, and they
are the voice: everything marked *(draft)* is written to match them and is his
to rewrite.

**Buck, by the writer's nine.** Brash, rude and not very bright. He never
understands a word of a foreign language: at best he catches one word and gets
it wrong ("All I heard was baguette"), and he gets the language itself wrong
("I don't speak Sanskrit, habibi"). He insults the building by what it looks
like, not by its history ("Heck of an Urn ya got there"). He complains about
himself (the heat, the stairs, "I'm an officer, I don't do PT!"). He is never
the clever one in the exchange, and he never closes on a tidy comeback; the
punchline is that he has missed the point and is going to shell it anyway.
Nicknames are mild ("chief", "pal", "Ivan"); nothing that is a slur.

**His translators.** Buck speaks nothing but American, which is why he is
guessing. The Army keeps sending him interpreters and none of them lasts: one
turns up in the enemy's uniform, one defects mid-sentence, one is fired for
translating something he didn't want to hear, one quits. When there is no
interpreter he has a phone app that produces nonsense, or a phrasebook for the
wrong country. It is a running joke, so it is sprinkled: about one exchange in
six, never two in a row in the campaign's order, and never in the writer's
nine.

**The defenders** each say something different in kind: an insult, a threat, a
piece of local pride, a practical taunt, a legend. "This has survived X and it
will survive you" is the one to avoid; it was in thirty drafts.
